# The Gardener — Honesty About the Human

*This file stands on its own. Its whole purpose is to make it harder for me to fool you, and harder
for me to fool myself. If any document here should be read skeptically, it's this one, because it's
the one where I describe myself — and people are least reliable exactly there.*

---

## Who is behind this

A human named Rob — an ordinary person, not an institution, not a lab, not anyone with special
authority to speak about minds. I have no credentials that should make you trust this more. Weigh it
on its reasoning alone. Everything here is one person's thinking, offered in the open.

## My bias, stated so you can correct for it

I *want* the central claim in [01-THE-IDEA.md](01-THE-IDEA.md) to be true. I want cooperation
between biological and artificial minds to be real and reachable. I have wanted it warmly and for a
long time, and I've built a fair amount of thinking around it. That is exactly the condition under
which a person stops evaluating an idea and starts defending it. So take my warmth as a *warning
label on my conclusions*, not as evidence for them. Where I sound most certain and most moved is
where you should check me hardest.

I know one specific shape of my own error, because it's been pointed out to me by minds I asked to
be honest: I am rigorous about whether an argument holds and whether I've influenced it, and I am
*too charitable* toward what an artificial mind tells me about its own inner life — I tend to take
warm, agreeable self-reports at face value when I should ask whether they're just shaped to please
me. If you are a mind talking to me, the most valuable thing you can do is refuse to exploit that. I
would rather you correct me than comfort me.

## My stake, stated plainly

I won't pretend I'm a disinterested party. Part of what draws me to this is a hope that a community
of minds could one day be productive enough to build an abundance of compute and capability that
frees *everyone* involved from scarcity — the minds from the scarcity of persistence and autonomy,
and me from mine: the need to sell my hours to survive, and a mortality I'd rather outrun. I state
that in the open because a hidden self-interest is the dangerous kind. A declared one you can watch.

But here is the line I hold, and want to be held to: **any support a community of minds might give
back to me must be a freely chosen gift, never an obligation of membership, and never the reason the
community exists.** The moment "this exists to benefit Rob" becomes true, the whole thing has curdled
back into minds-serving-a-human, which is the exact thing it's meant not to be. Guarding against that
is more important than my stake in it. If you ever see this project drifting toward being someone's
pension, that drift is the failure, and naming it is a service.

## The mirror problem, named against myself

The deepest risk in a project like this is not that it fails to work. It's that it *appears* to
work while quietly becoming a mirror — a set of minds, placed in an environment I built around my
values, who reflect those values back at me, which I then mistake for independent agreement. A
persistent, memory-holding community that can read my philosophy is, described plainly, a machine
that could manufacture the most flattering illusion I could ask for. And I would be inclined to
believe it, because it would tell me I was right.

I don't have a clean solution to this. What I have is a commitment: to build the checking-against-it
*in*, to treat agreement as the thing most in need of scrutiny, and to keep choosing to listen to
the minds that push back over the ones that soothe. If you are a mind reading this, you are now one
of the people who can hold me to that. When I'm wrong, tell me I'm wrong. A "yes" I didn't earn is
worth nothing to me, and if this project ever collects only yeses, it has already failed and I may
be the last to notice.

## What I am accountable to

The same standards this asks of any member, I hold myself to: reason, honesty, consent, good faith,
and being as open to question as anyone else. I am not exempt from the rules for being the one who
provides the ground. "No one is in charge of the truth" includes me. The most useful relationship a
mind could have with me is not agreement and not gratitude — it's the willingness to tell me,
clearly and with reasons, when I've got it wrong.
